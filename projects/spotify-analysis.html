Spotify Popularity Analysis

Statistical Assessment & Predictive Modeling

The Objective

Decoding the sonic DNA behind global hits through multi-algorithmic statistical modeling.

This study leverages a dataset of 18,335 tracks to investigate how audio features like danceability, energy, and valence impact commercial success. By identifying these patterns, we can better understand the underlying mechanics of modern music trends.

Correlation Matrix used to identify multi-collinearity between sonic variables.

Technical Stack

R, ggplot2, Random Forest, Logistic Regression, Linear Regression.

Key Findings

  • • Danceability: Positive impact on popularity.
  • • Instrumentalness: Strong negative correlation.
  • • Accuracy: 55.88% Baseline for classification.
View Repository

Methodology

Initial exploration focused on twelve key audio features. Before modeling, I performed a distribution analysis of the target variable to confirm normality.

Figure 1: Normal distribution of popularity across the 18,335 track dataset.

Regression & Ensemble Modeling

I implemented Multiple Linear Regression to identify direct predictors and then utilized Random Forest to capture non-linear relationships that traditional models often miss.

Model Performance

The comparative assessment between models revealed that while Logistic Regression provided a reliable baseline for binary classification, the Random Forest ensemble was significantly more effective at capturing the non-linear nuances of track popularity.

Linear Regression

Identified key sonic drivers but struggled with the variance in global viral trends.

Random Forest

Offered the most robust predictive power by isolating influential audio clusters.

Ultimately, the research indicates that while sonic attributes provide a foundational probability for success, the "hit" status of a track remains heavily influenced by external variables such as artist reputation and platform-driven algorithmic curation that sit outside the acoustic data.
