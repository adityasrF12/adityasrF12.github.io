Real-Time Drowsiness Detection

Computer Vision • Deep Learning • YOLOv8

The Objective

Engineering a real-time defense against driver fatigue through high-frequency Computer Vision.

Drowsy driving accounts for thousands of accidents annually, often occurring in split-seconds of inattention. The goal of this project was to develop a non-invasive, autonomous monitoring system capable of detecting the earliest physiological signs of exhaustion—such as microsleeps and yawning—with near-perfect precision.

By leveraging state-of-the-art object detection architectures, this system transforms a standard camera feed into a proactive safety layer, capable of triggering life-saving alerts before a fatigue-related incident occurs.

State: Active
State: Fatigue

Automated classification of behavioral indicators: open vs. closed eyes and focused vs. fatigued expressions.

Technical Stack

YOLOv5, YOLOv8, OpenCV, PyTorch.

Architecture & Methodology

The system utilizes YOLOv5 for initial facial feature detection and YOLOv8 for high-speed real-time inference. The models were fine-tuned on a custom dataset of 9,120 images categorized into Active and Fatigue states, ensuring robustness across diverse lighting and face orientations.

Model Reliability

Confusion Matrix verifying high precision in identifying fatigue-related behaviors.

By achieving a precision score of 0.976, the system minimizes false positives, which is critical for maintaining driver trust in real-world safety applications.

Performance Validation

Real-time testing with OpenCV confirmed the model's ability to localize fatigue indicators—such as eye closure and yawning—within live video feeds with minimal latency.
